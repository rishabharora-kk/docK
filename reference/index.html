Pick up. Move aside. Put down.

Drag sideways to reorder. Drag up to remove. Undo brings it back. Keyboard: Space to pick up, arrows to move, Space to drop. Escape cancels. Delete removes.

Try it. Drag an icon.
Explore the interaction design States, motion tokens & edge cases

State machine

Seven states, one active at a time. Every path ends in Idle, so the dock can never get stuck.

Why it feels right

  1. The icon is glued to the pointer. Position has zero lag, so the hand always trusts it. All personality lives in scale, tilt and shadow.
  2. Everything else is a spring. On release, the pointer’s velocity carries into the settle, so the drop continues the gesture instead of starting a new animation.
  3. Space opens before it’s asked for. Neighbours part early and a faint outline shows where the icon will land.
  4. Removal is shown by losing weight (smaller, lighter), never by red or alarms. It is always undoable.
  5. One gesture, one meaning. Keyboard, Esc, touch cancel and reduced motion are first-class paths, not add-ons.

Motion tokens

Press
Scale 0.92, spring 300/26. Acknowledges contact immediately.
Arm to lift
130 ms hold, or 6 px of movement, whichever comes first. Mouse users who just drag never wait.
Lift
Scale 1.14, rises 6 px, shadow deepens, spring 600/36. A short haptic tick on touch devices.
Tilt
0.012 degrees per px/s of horizontal speed, capped at 10 degrees. Fast hands give a looser icon.
Reorder
Swap only when the icon passes 0.62 of a slot, so slots never flicker at boundaries. Neighbours spring 520/34.
Remove intent
52 px above the dock to enter, 28 px to leave. Scale 0.82, opacity 0.72, gap closes, label says “Release to remove”.
Drop and settle
Spring 520/34 (damping ratio about 0.75): one small overshoot, then rest.
Dock width
Spring 300/30, always centred, so the dock breathes instead of jumping.
Undo
Four seconds, or Ctrl/Cmd+Z. The icon returns to its original slot.

Edge cases handled

  • Tap without lifting: the icon hops once (launch feedback) and nothing reorders.
  • Grab an icon mid-settle: allowed. Springs keep their velocity, so the hand-off is continuous.
  • Second finger or second pointer during a drag: ignored.
  • Touch cancelled, window loses focus, or Esc: the icon returns to its original slot.
  • Dragged far below or to the sides of the dock: it follows with rubber-band resistance and the order still resolves from its centre.
  • Reduced motion: springs become near-instant, tilt is off, hierarchy and states stay identical.
  • Screen readers: each step is announced with position, for example “Picked up Notes, position 3 of 6”.
